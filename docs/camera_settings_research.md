# Camera settings: what the planner's rules are based on

Sources were read on 2026-10-27.

## ISO (sensor sensitivity)
- Higher ISO amplifies the signal, and with it the noise: grain in shadows and blotchy colour. Modern cameras handle ISO 3200 to 6400 well; phone sensors show noise much sooner.
- Source: [PhotoWorkout, Exposure Triangle Explained](https://www.photoworkout.com/?p=566)

## Shutter speed
- Below about 1/30 s, moving subjects streak and hand-held shots risk shake.
- Handheld minimum: keep the shutter at or above 1 over the focal length (1/200 s at 200 mm). Stabilisation adds margin but does not stop subject motion.
- Use 1/1000 s or faster to freeze moving subjects.
- Source: [PhotoWorkout](https://www.photoworkout.com/?p=566)

## White balance (colour temperature, Kelvin)
- Daylight about 5500 to 6500 K; cloudy and shade about 7000 to 10000 K; tungsten (warm bulbs) about 2500 to 3500 K; fluorescent about 4000 to 5000 K.
- A setting lower than the light's real temperature leaves a cool, blue cast; a higher one leaves a warm, yellow cast (this is standard colour-temperature behaviour; the source gave the Kelvin values only).
- Source: [SLRLounge, Kelvin white balance](https://slrlounge.com/glossary/kelvin-white-balance-setting-definition)

## Composition
- Rule of thirds: place the subject on a line or where two lines cross. Source: [Fotor, aesthetic photos](https://fotor.com/blog/how-to-make-aesthetic-photos)

## What the planner does with these
- Night sky and moon: ISO 1600 for dark sky (1/30 s, the handheld floor); ISO 400 and 1/250 s for bright moon or lights; daylight white balance; focus at infinity; hold still or use a tripod, and stack the 5 frames.
- Bright or backlit sky: ISO 100 and 1/500 s to protect highlights.
- Dim room: ISO 400 and 1/30 s, never slower than the handheld floor.
- Ordinary daylight: ISO 200 and 1/250 s.
